Unit I
Literary Criticism
Multiplicity of Perspective
A prism does not change the light — it reveals what was always inside it. Each critical lens does the same to a text. Meaning always depends on the perspective you bring.
"Analyzing Little Red Riding Hood through different lenses showed how much I had missed. What the Marxist lens saw, the Jungian lens did not — and both interpretations were completely valid."

Confidence of Voice
Tone, pacing, and personality are not extras. They ARE the argument. Delivery shapes how an idea lands in the world.

Unit III
Drama
Form as Meaning
Miller uses non-realistic staging — overlapping timelines, flashbacks of Ben, past and present colliding — to externalize Willy's psychological collapse. The structure of the play IS the diagnosis.
"When Howard fires Willy while Ben appears beside him — the lighting shift showed Ben was not real. In the text it was ambiguous. On screen it was devastating. Stage directions reveal the subtext beneath the dialogue."

Unit IV
Novel Study
Silence as Expression
Findley uses fragmentation, silence, and restraint to convey the psychological cost of war. Robert refusing to abandon the horses communicates trauma more powerfully than any description ever could.
"The book club showed me how differently readers interpret the same text. I had settled on one reading — the discussion revealed Findley deliberately leaves Robert's final act ambiguous. Heroic or tragic. Genuinely unresolved."
